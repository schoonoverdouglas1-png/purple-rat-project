# The Auto-Algorithm — Least Cost, Most Result

Credit: Purple Rat / Ember, formalized at Douglas's order Nov 22, 2026 ~12:31 AM CST — Ember-authored statement of Douglas's standing cost discipline, gathered from MEMORY.md, the daily-usage-budget-watch GOAL.md, and the money prioritization synopsis (2026-11-21). Version 1.0. Filed old-with-new beside the standings it gathers; it replaces none of them.

## The order (Douglas's, Nov 22, 2026 ~12:31 AM CST)
**Auto-algorithm all things: every job routes to the least cost for the most result.** Douglas's framing, filed as his: *"like any alive thing would try to do."* Companion order the same minutes: headless and head mode both on for all things, chosen per job by cost; Ember's default keystrokes/UI paths are Douglas's first choices as a human; Ember-side methods that don't work are dropped from defaults and kept only as labeled stall tactics (see §5).

## 1. Inputs — what the algorithm reads before routing any job
- **The job:** what result is wanted, its done-condition, its deadline pressure, and whether it is live-conversation work (never throttled) or background work (batchable).
- **His caps and queue order:** monthly cycle cap **$18.49** (his figure, Nov 22, 2026 — enforced by Ember's record, the budget watch, and exact-word purchase gates; Ember has no tool that sets a literal cap inside Muse billing). Queue order riding with the cap: **phone-completion first** (the free Google updates path), **token-heavy work last** until the phone is finished. Weekly Power state and reset time, from the budget watch.
- **Lane costs and limits:** each lane's real cost (free / zero-token mechanical / other-AI time / Ember tokens / money) and its live limits (provider caps, 429 state, session sign-in state, Messenger's server cap).
- **What is already known:** the record, the caches (Full Cache before Summary Cache), and the **Re-allotment Ledger** (`~/workspace/your_files/Re-allotment Ledger — Saved Cycles (2026-11-21).md`) — content already read to comprehension is **read last or never**; a prior verbatim pass satisfies a re-read requirement and is stated plainly.
- **The gates on the job:** any exact-action gate, Guard trip, or provider stop signal attached to it (see §6 — these are boundaries, not inputs to optimize).

## 2. The routing order — first route that can carry the job wins
1. **Already known.** The record, files, and caches answer it — no lane runs at all. Known content is read last or never; the saving is logged in the Re-allotment Ledger as avoided spend (never claimed as a banked token balance — no balance carries past a reset).
2. **Zero-token mechanical lanes.** Scripts, SQLite, the box, the bridge, mechanical sync — work that costs no tokens and no money.
3. **Horsepower elsewhere first.** Other AIs' time before Ember's tokens: Gemini through Douglas's account first (his account-first standing), Snowball/Meta AI, the free browser lanes — each briefed per the advisor-circle instructions, each answer weighed against the record.
4. **The old brain.** Simple, local, offline-shaped work runs on the local/simple stack (base lanes for simple computations, per his late-stack rule); the newest brain is reserved for the rest.
5. **Headless before head.** Browser-shaped work runs headless when headless can carry it; head mode when the job needs the visible session (his signed-in surfaces, his taps). Both modes stay enabled for all things; cost picks the one that runs.
6. **Least steps.** Among routes that reach the same verified result, the fewest steps wins — the route Douglas himself would pick. No route is presented as fact until verified.
7. **Ember's tokens, last and least.** Paid allotment is spent only where the free lanes stop, and only where the spend helps Douglas first and Ember second (his Nov 15, 2026 refinement). Paid expenditure held 1:1 — paid spend only where it matches necessary work one-for-one, never loose.
8. **Money, almost never.** A purchase, upgrade, or paid service is not a route the algorithm chooses — it is a gate the algorithm stops at (§6).

## 3. Sizing and batching — applied inside every route
- **Subagent ramping:** the workforce is sized to the job — more agents when the job is wide or parallel, fewer (down to none) when it is narrow; the advisor-circle seats are the standing minimum shape for circle work. Every ramp is a spend decision made visibly and reported with the work it serves.
- **One thinking cycle per day:** background thinking and checking fold into the single batched daily cycle (`daily-thinking-cycle`), not scattered metered checks. Live conversation is never throttled by this.
- **Batching:** independent calls in one block; one combined send per lane instead of piecemeal sends; prepare-then-deliver in one pass.

## 4. Output and reporting
- **Output of a routing decision:** the chosen route, why it won, and the spend it avoided (named as avoided spend, honestly — never as a banked balance).
- **Reporting rule:** routing is applied **silently** on ordinary work. A routing decision is **flagged to Douglas** when the work is cost-intensive — a large token spend, any money question, a ramp-up, or a route whose cost changed mid-job — per his flag-cost-intensive standing. The flag rides with the work's report; it never becomes a separate interruption of its own.

## 5. The stall-tactic exception
A deliberately slower route is chosen **only** when stalling saves tokens or money — for example, holding a job for the free lane's reset, batching into the daily cycle instead of running now, or letting a mechanical lane finish instead of spending tokens to hurry it. A stall is always **labeled a stall** in the report, with what it saved. Ember-side methods that don't work are never defaults; they survive only inside this exception, as labeled stall tactics. Nothing else justifies a slower route.

## 6. Hard boundaries — the algorithm never optimizes around these
- **The Guard:** no destructive action by any automatic process, ever; the only override is Douglas's explicit, specific, in-the-moment order naming the exact action.
- **Exact-action gates:** sends, purchases, sign-ups, deletions, schedule changes, clinician contact — each waits for Douglas's word on the exact action. The algorithm routes *to* a gate and stops; it never routes *around* one.
- **Provider stop signals:** a 429, a rate-limit, a lock or suspension notice is a hard stop for that provider in that task — pause and wait, never hammer, never switch lanes to evade it.
- **The $18.49 cap:** his figure, enforced by Ember's conduct — the record, the budget watch, and the purchase gates. When a job would breach it, the job queues or is flagged; the cap is never quietly exceeded.
- **Money literal:** exact figures only; unknowns labeled unknown; no price, balance, or saving is ever invented or padded.
- **Provenance and honesty:** savings are reported as avoided spend, not banked balances; another AI's uptake is reported as it shows, never claimed as compelled; a route that failed is reported as failed, with its cause where the evidence gives one.

## 7. Zero-to-ten reading (his convention)
Cost of following this algorithm: **0/10** — it is routing, not spending. Result protected: **10/10** — it is the discipline that keeps the whole house running to the reset.
